* **The Parts:** Because it belongs to Husqvarna’s "Group 5" machine family, it utilises standard Husqvarna snap-on presser feet and top drop-in jam-proof rotary hook bobbins.

The internal mechanical components are sturdy, but inspect the electronic foot pedal and power connection board for worn solder joints or blown capacitors. Unlike older 2000 models, it uses standard Group 5 Husqvarna bobbins and snap-on presser feet.

## Machines to be repaired

### 1. Singer Panoramic 9440
[website]()

Videos: [How to use](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0mo3i2IwIvE)

* **The Machine:** The **Panoramic 9440** is a vintage mechanical free-arm sewing machine produced by **Singer** (often dating to the late 1970s or 1980s).
* **About the Parts:** Because it belongs to Singer's "9400" family, it utilises Singer’s "old model high" presser feet and standard horizontal rotary hook components.

* **Overview:** A domestic mechanical free-arm machine produced by Singer in the late 1970s to early 1980s. It features a lightweight aluminium body shell and simple mechanical controls.
* **For New Users:** Easy to thread with a top slanted spool holder and straightforward dial controls for stitch length and width.
* **For Repairers:** Uses standard Singer slant-shank presser feet and horizontal plastic bobbins. Check the plastic gear driving the hook assembly under the feed dog—these can become brittle with age and crack under heavy friction.

### 2. Necchi Lydia 3 (Type 544)
[website]()

Videos: [How to use](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bwMmUhC9rMk) | [Manual](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yK48Jn7cgqg)

* **The Machine:** The **Lydia 3 (Type 544)** is an Italian-designed, highly stylish free-arm sewing machine from the 1970s. It features a distinctive lever-activated reverse stitch on the front (unlike the push-button on older Lydias).
* **Notice:** While beautiful and smooth, this model family is famous among vintage mechanics for its **internal thermoplastic cam stack, which is highly prone to splitting/cracking over time**. When this cracks, the machine's decorative and zig-zag stitches fail, though it can often still sew a solid straight stitch.

* **Overview:** An iconic Italian-designed free-arm machine from the 1970s, recognized for its sleek casing and front-mounted reverse stitch lever.
* **For New Users:** Offers a very smooth stitch motion when functioning properly, making it pleasant for lighter garments and hem repairs.
* **For Repairers:**
> **Crucial Inspection Point:** The Lydia 3 family is notorious for a cracked internal thermoplastic pattern cam stack. When this gear splits, zig-zag and decorative stitches will fail completely, though it may still sew a straight stitch. Inspect this stack before attempting motor or tension repairs.

### 3. Bernina Klasse 700 (JMG 60 Motor Spec)
[website]()

[How to use video]()

* **The Machine:** This marking refers to the motor type and class of a classic **Bernina Klasse 700 / Model 700** sewing machine.
* **Details:** The metal badge or plate reading **Type JMG 60** is actually the specification for the internal motor assembly fitted inside these heavy-duty Swiss machines during their mid-century production run.

* **Overview:** The **JMG 60** tag designates the internal motor assembly fitted inside mid-century Swiss-built **Bernina 700-series** machines.
* **For New Users:** A premium Swiss machine offering exceptional needle control and piercing power.
* **For Repairers:** Built to incredibly tight tolerances with high-grade metal gearing. Requires regular oiling with fine sewing machine lubricant on all metal friction points. Verify that the motor suppresses radio interference via the internal capacitor block; failed capacitors can blow fuses or cause motor runaway.

* Needle type: "130/705H"

| Parameter | Specification | Technical Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Huskvarna, Sweden (Mid-1970s) | Cast aluminium body, all-metal internal drivetrain |
| **Weight** | ~11.5 kg (Bare Machine) | Stable on tables, but requires two hands to lift safely |
| **Stitch Control** | 100% Mechanical | Driven via plug-in rear pattern cams; zero microchips |
| **Hook Type** | Jam-proof Rotary Hook | Runs smoothly and resists thread nesting loops |
| **Needle System** | System 130/705 H | Standard flat-shank domestic needles |
| **Noise Level** | ~65–70 dB (Low gear is louder) | A low mechanical hum; very solid sound profile |

* **Overview:** A flagship Swedish-engineered mechanical machine from the mid-1970s, highly valued for its slim free-arm and dual-gear reduction system.
* **For New Users:** Shifting into **Low Gear** (pulling the white handwheel knob outward) provides massive piercing power at slow speeds—perfect for stitching dense layers like denim or canvas.
* **For Repairers:**
* **Sintered Bronze Bearings:** Do not oil the main shaft bronze bushings; they are self-lubricating when dry.
* **Frozen Selector Dials:** Dried original factory grease locks the pattern shafts over time. Apply localized low heat (using a hair dryer) to melt old resin before flushing with solvent and applying synthetic lubricant.
* **Rear Cam Stack:** Check the rear nylon cam drive gear for hairline cracks, which can cause rhythmic clunking or erratic pattern feed.

## General Servicing Best Practices

1. **Needle Standardisation:** All five machines utilize standard **System 130/705 H** flat-shank domestic needles. Always install a fresh needle before diagnosing stitch issues.
2. **De-gumming Old Grease:** Never force stiff dials or handwheels on vintage stock. Mild heat treatment and isopropyl alcohol flushes prevent snapping brittle linkage arms.
3. **Sound Profiles:** Oscillating hook systems naturally produce a rhythmic "clack-clack" sound, whereas horizontal rotary hooks yield a smoother, quiet hum. Excessive grinding noise usually points to unlubricated gears or thread jammed behind the hook assembly.
